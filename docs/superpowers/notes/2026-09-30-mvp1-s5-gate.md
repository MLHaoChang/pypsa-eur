## 2. The by-construction identity: exact, for PV too

**The claim.** NPV = (LP objective saving) × AF(7 %, 25).

**Numeric check.** My independent (savings − LP annual charge of the option's assets) × AF equals `(nb.objective − n.objective) × AF` to within 1e-13 relative, for **both** options:

| Option | (savings − LP annual) × AF | LP objective saving × AF |
|---|---|---|
| `bess_2h` | 371,681.602775077 | 371,681.602775189 |
| `bess_pv_2h` | 1,566,950.761638796 | 1,566,950.761638610 |

**Why it holds.** At the ledger rate, each upfront purchase discounts exactly to the annuities the LP charged over the life it serves inside the horizon. The salvage is the present value of the annuities that fall after the horizon.
- *Inverter.* The purchases at 0, 10 and 20, less v²⁵·a₁₀·AF₅ at year 25, equal a₁₀·AF₂₅ exactly: 1.659208070745 on both sides.
  - This works for any whole-year inverter life. For a life of 12, the purchases fall at 0, 12 and 24 and 11 years remain: 1.467209297 on both sides.
  - If the inverter life is at least the horizon, there is no replacement and the same rule applies.
- *Storage block.* Its life equals the horizon by definition, so the upfront cost equals a₂₅·AF₂₅.
- *PV.* capex − v²⁵·a₄₀·AF₁₅ = a₄₀·AF₂₅, which is 0.874125238993 on both sides. The pack writes the PV's `discount_rate` as the ledger rate (`packs.build_site_network`).
- *FOM and VOM.* Both the LP and the cash flow book them at the end of each of years 1–25.
- *Savings.* They equal the LP's operating-cost delta:
  - the energy, network and export prices are `marginal_cost` on the Links;
  - the LP's demand-charge variable equals the monthly maximum at the optimum;
  - the fixed and contracted-capacity charges are identical in both bills.

**Where it would break**, and whether each case is guarded:

| Break | Effect | Guarded? |
|---|---|---|
| PV `discount_rate` ≠ ledger rate | Salvage and discounting are on different rates | Not refused. `pv_rate` is read from the generator and used silently. The pack prevents it today. [N3] |
| PV life < horizon | A replacement would be needed | Refused (`lifetime_shorter_than_horizon`) |
| A non-whole-year life | Rounding | Refused (`lifetime_not_whole_years`) |
| Ledger edited after the run (rate, cost or life baked into `capital_cost`) | The case prices a ledger the LP never saw | The route returns 409 on the ledger hash. The function itself only compares `cfg.discount_rate`, which the route rebuilds from the same ledger, so inside the route this check is unreachable. It is defence-in-depth for S6. |
| **A fork changed after the run** | Sizes from the new network, bills from the old run | **Not guarded: see [B1]** |
| Solver tolerance | Demand-charge peak variable against the bill's max | At 1e-6 relative, as the test pins |
| S6 re-dispatch at fixed sizes | The identity no longer applies, so NPV can go negative | By design (B5) |

**[B2 / binding] `npv_nonnegative_approximate_with_pv` is wrong.**
- The identity is exact for `bess_pv` as well. Once salvage is on the annuity basis, the only approximation left is solver tolerance, which the battery-only case has too.
- The plan's BC-7 wording ("stated as approximate for `bess_pv`") was written before the salvage basis was pinned. The implementation followed the plan.
- The note undersells the construction. It invites a reader to treat the PV option's positive NPV as partly independent evidence, when it is not.
- Required:
  - emit `npv_nonnegative_at_optimum_by_construction` for every option;
  - drop the "approximate" code;
  - flip the assertion in `test_npv_is_the_lp_saving_annuitised_by_construction`;
  - amend the S5 text of the plan (and BC-7's row) to say "exact for every option on the annuity salvage basis".

**Is the NPV honestly presented? Yes, with the note, and only because S6's verdict does not read its sign.**
- At the optimum, the NPV restates the LP objective saving. It is non-negative by construction.
- The same holds for the other two headline KPIs:
  - whenever CAPEX > 0, IRR ≥ r (the root is unique on this fixture);
  - the discounted payback is ≤ the horizon, because the cumulative discounted cash flow ends at NPV ≥ 0.
- These are the three numbers the client reads. Carry to S6/S7: the verdict page's by-construction disclosure must cover **NPV, IRR ≥ the discount rate and discounted payback ≤ the horizon**, not NPV alone.
- The magnitudes are informative. The signs are not.

## 5. XLSX

**[OK] The NPV cell** is exactly `=J5+NPV($B$1,J6:J30)` on the fixture (column J is the net cash flow; row 5 is year 0; row 30 is year 25), which is `=CF0+NPV(rate,CF1:CFn)`.
- The KPIs sheet's NPV cell is a reference to it, not a copy of the number.
- The discount factor, discounted cash flow and cumulative cash flow are all formulas.
- There is a `SUM` check row.
- Implementer mutation m5 (the range starting at CF0) turns the test red.

**[OK] The evaluator matches Excel's documented examples.** Microsoft's NPV page gives:
- `NPV(10%,-10000,3000,4200,6800)` = 1,188.44;
- `NPV(8%,B3:B7)+B2` = 1,922.06 (I recomputed 1,922.0616);
- the same with −9,000 appended = −3,749.47.

It also follows Excel's semantics:
- NPV discounts its first value one period;
- empty cells are skipped inside NPV ranges and read as 0 in arithmetic;
- `^` binds tighter than `/`, and unary minus binds tighter than `^`;
- `^` is left-associative;
- it refuses any function it does not implement (IRR is tested).

It is stricter than Excel on text in a referenced cell (it raises), which is acceptable.

**[OK] Formula injection.** In XLSX, only a string starting with `=` becomes a formula: openpyxl sets `data_type='f'` only for that prefix. Strings starting with `+`, `-` or `@` are stored as inert strings. The CSV-style risk does not apply to a stored string cell (the ledger CSV has its own guard, from S2). `_text` forces `data_type='s'` for `=` strings, and the test reloads the file and asserts `s`. My mutation M6 turns it red; see the table.

**[OK] Provenance sheet.** It carries:

- the library version, ledger hash, model hash, project ref and tariff id;

**What it does not exercise:**

**[S2 / real finding, carry to S6, S7 and S9] The shipped sector profiles size a battery-only option to zero.** I solved `bess_2h` for every shipped profile against every seed tariff (`scratchpad/s5gate/office.py` and `zero.py`):

|---|---|---|
| `commercial_office` | 0 | 0 |
| `industrial_two_shift` | 0 | 0 |

In every case p_nom_opt = −0.0 and the objective saving is 0.0.

This is economically plausible, not a bug:
- a 2 h battery costs about 64k EUR/MW/yr in annuities;
- the demand charge is worth 108k EUR/MW/yr;
- so a battery pays only if it can shave at least about 0.6 MW per MW, that is, if the peak plateau is shorter than about 3.4 h;
- the office peaks are broad plateaus, and smooth synthetic profiles also lack the short spikes real meters show.

But it defines what a novice sees. **Anyone on the default (sector-profile) path gets "no battery" for every battery-only duration.**

With the office profile, `bess_pv_2h` builds 0.32 MW of battery and 2.22 MW of PV:
- NPV is 1.15M;
- the energy stream is 68 %, the demand stream only 13 %;
- **the question has no PV-only option**, so the combined NPV cannot be attributed to the battery.

The report must state all three:
- the synthetic profile understates peak-shaving value (`synthetic_load_profile` exists as a code; the report needs the sentence);
- the battery-only options sized to zero;
- the `bess_pv` value is mostly PV. S6 needs a PV-only reference or an explicit "not attributable to the battery" disclosure before the verdict names a BESS.

**Zero-size case (office, `bess_2h`), `status=ok`:**

This is honest. But the case still carries `inverter_replaced_at_its_lifetime`, `battery_upfront_from_ledger_not_back_calculated` and `salvage_annuity_pv_remaining_life`, which say nothing at zero size. Add a `size_zero_no_investment` code. [N8]
