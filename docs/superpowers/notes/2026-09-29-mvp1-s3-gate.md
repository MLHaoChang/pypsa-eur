## Summary

The phase does what the plan asks, and its core claims reproduce:

- **The wrapper is correct.** It builds the `peak_import` variable over `billing_period`, the `peak_import_le` constraint on Σ `Link-p` over the import links, and the objective term. It sits at the specified point in the chain.
- **The no-op proof is real.** The hashes were recomputed from HEAD~1 through `run_simulation` and are identical. The always-active mutation turns the test red.
- **The bridge closes.** The residual is 9.46e-11 percent. The bill's `demand` equals the bridge's `demand_charge_eur` exactly, and the bill's energy + network + export credit equals the link OPEX to the cent.
- **The prices are real network data.** They survive the product's netCDF save, and `n.statistics().opex()` sees them.
- **The S1 carry-overs are enforced and tested:** first-match-wins bands, refusal of unpriced hours, export priced one way or the other, and refusal of `annual_peak` and `ratchet`.

Two defects must be fixed in this phase:

1. The new preflight builds a dense marginal-cost frame for every Link on every LOPF preflight: 3 s and 631 MB at 3000 links, with no pair present at all.
2. The bill charges a `measured` capacity charge on the horizon's **annual** peak, which the LP never sees. That is the `annual_peak` basis under another name, and the S1 gate required it refused.

Neither changes an existing number, and both are cheap to fix.

### (2) The bill calculator (`services/study/tariff.py::BillCalculator.bill`)

**[OK] Band precedence and unpriced hours.**
- First match wins, through `& np.isnan(price)`.
- An unpriced hour raises `UnpricedHoursError`, both in the bill and in `write_tariff_prices`.
- Prices are validated before any figure is computed, so a refused tariff is refused whichever series are present.
- `test_first_matching_band_wins` covers both orders.

**[OK] Export pricing is one or the other.** Giving both raises `ExportPricingError`. Giving neither is refused on write. In the bill, giving neither gives null with `no_export_price`, unless nothing was exported, which is a real 0. A `series_ref` that does not resolve or does not cover every snapshot is refused. `cap_mw` is refused (`export_cap_unsupported`), which is honest.

**[OK] Monthly peaks and weightings.** There is one peak per billing period, unweighted: a peak is MW, not MWh. Energy is weighted by `snapshot_weightings`, which `test_snapshot_weightings_scale_energy_not_peaks` covers.

**[OK] Nulls, flags and `annual_bill`.**
- Every figure is null with a flag through `_FigureBlock` (ADR-0001).
- `total` is null with `component_unavailable` when any component is null.
- `annual_bill` equals `total` only at 8760 or 8784 hours. Otherwise it is null with `horizon_not_one_year` and is never extrapolated. I checked this on a real 8760-hour solve.

**[OK] Partly covered periods.** A partly covered period is charged in full, for both the fixed and the demand charge. I checked this with a horizon from 15 January to 10 February: the fixed charge is 2 × 400 and the demand charge is 2 × 1000. The behaviour is stated in a note (see N2 on how the note is worded).

**[OK] The bridge (`objective_decomposition._bridge`).** `demand_charge_eur` is recomputed from `links_t.p0` through `BillCalculator.demand_charge`, and `n.model` is never read.
- It is added to `lp_basis_total` **after** `period_weighting_adjustment_eur` is computed, so the adjustment is intact: −3.2e-6 EUR on the toy.
- Residual: 4.8e-6 EUR, or 9.46e-11 percent.
- The bill's `demand` equals the bridge's value exactly.

I applied the mutation that drops `peak_import_le`. The decomposition test goes red, with a residual of about −1.0 MEUR: the bridge detects an LP that did not carry the term. That is the property we want.

### (3) `write_tariff_prices`

**[OK] Permanent network data.** The prices are written to `n.links_t.marginal_cost`: positive on import, negative (the credit) on export. They are never reverted. They survive `PyPSAService.export_network_to_netcdf`, which is the product's one save path, and a reload; I checked this on a solved network, and the test checks plain netCDF.

`n.statistics.opex()` for the Links is 3,908,205.17, equal to Σ p0 × mc. `cost_breakdown.opex` carries the same figure.

**[OK] The per-MWh network charge.** It is folded into the import price in the LP, which is correct because it is a real marginal import cost. It is **not** double counted in the bill: `energy` prices the band only, and `network` prices the per-MWh adder plus the per-period and per-year terms. Energy + network + export credit equals the link OPEX exactly.

The only way to double count it is a later phase adding `cost_breakdown.opex` to the bill, which S5 already forbids ("never system `cost_breakdown.opex`").

### (5) The preflight warning (`validation_service._check_export_cycling`)

The logic is right for the same hour. A pair of opposite Links triggers a warning when −mc_back × η_fwd − mc_fwd > 0. The test covers both the warning and the clean case.

**[S1] (binding, BC-S3-1) The check is O(all Links × snapshots) on every LOPF preflight, for every project.**

`n.get_switchable_as_dense("Link", "marginal_cost")` builds the full dense frame before it looks for any reverse pair. It is the only such dense call in `validate_for_run`.

I measured it on 3000 unpaired Links × 8760 snapshots: **3.0 s and 631 MB peak** to return `[]`. A PyPSA-Eur-sized network, with about 10k Links, would allocate about 2 GB in the preflight of every solve. That is a regression for existing projects, even though no number changes.

The fix:
- Find the reverse pairs from `bus0` and `bus1` first, and return early when there are none.
- Densify only the paired Links.
- Add a test that a network with no reverse pair never calls `get_switchable_as_dense`, for example with a monkeypatched sentinel.

**[S2] (not binding; S4 should pick it up) Cross-hour cycling through the battery is not caught.**

The docstring says the check covers "a battery charges from the grid to export", but it compares prices in the same hour only. A time-of-use import band of 30 with a flat export of 40 passes the check, yet a battery would import at 30 and export at 40 × η for profit.

The plan's wording ("in any hour") is met. The S4 seed-tariff acceptance ("export price below import price in every hour") has the same blind spot. Add a second warning when max(export credit) × round-trip efficiency exceeds min(import price) and storage is present. Alternatively, S4 can assert max export < min import on the seeds.

**[S3] (binding, BC-S3-2) The `measured` capacity charge is an annual-peak charge the LP never sees.**

The problem:
- `BillCalculator.bill` prices `CapacityCharge(basis="measured")` as `price × imp.max() × years`, which is a charge on the horizon's **peak**.
- The LP has no such term, so battery sizing ignores it, while savings (`bill_baseline − bill_option`) would still count whatever peak reduction happens by accident.
- That is the `annual_peak` basis the S1 gate required S3 to refuse, arriving through a different field.
- Neither seed tariff uses it today, but the `Tariff` contract and the S2 library parser (`library.py`, `capacity_charge`) accept it.

The fix is one of:
- refuse `capacity_charge.basis == "measured"` in MVP-1 with a typed `UnsupportedTariffError` (`capacity_charge_measured_unsupported`), in the bill and in `write_tariff_prices`; or
- model it as a `billing_period="year"` demand charge.

Either way, add a unit test for the `capacity` component: `contracted` with a limit, `contracted` without a limit (null with `no_contracted_mw`), and `measured` refused. The `capacity` component currently has **no** test at all.

**[S4] (not binding; S4 must pick it up) Two sources for the billing period.** The LP reads `SolverConfig.demand_charge["billing_period"]`, while the bill reads `Tariff.billing_period`, and nothing ties them together.

S4 needs one helper, for example `demand_charge_config_from_tariff(tariff, import_links)` in `tariff.py`, and a runner test that the bill's `demand` equals the decomposition's `demand_charge_eur` on a solved fork.

**[S5] (not binding; S5/S7) Honesty notes are sentences, not codes.** `Bill.honesty_notes` contains prose such as "capacity charge pro-rated by horizon hours / 8760".
- The plan's vocabulary is snake_case codes (S5's list).
- S7's `validate_prose` rejects digits outside `{{fact_id}}`, so this string would trip it if a report renders it.
- The partial-period note is emitted whenever a fixed or demand charge exists, even when every period is fully covered.

Switch to codes such as `billing_period_partly_covered_charged_in_full` (emitted only when a period is actually partial) and `capacity_charge_prorated_by_hours`, before S4 consumes them.

**[S6] (not binding; S4) Non-unit or aggregated snapshot weightings are neither refused nor flagged.** A flat but resampled network, such as a 3-hourly one from the Snapshots panel, passes the flat-`DatetimeIndex` check. The LP and the bill then both price the peak of averaged power, which understates the demand charge.

The S4 pack sets weightings to 1, so the MVP-1 flow is safe. The wrapper should still at least emit a `[TARIFF]` warning, and the bill a flag, when `snapshot_weightings.objective` is not 1 everywhere.
