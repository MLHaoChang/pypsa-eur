# Campus visual layers — viability of the schematic, map and 3D views

Date: 2026-10-06
Scope: the owner's requirement for an integrated graphical representation of a campus in three layers — (1) an abstract canvas of the energy-flow infrastructure, (2) a map view on real coordinates with a draggable, real-world topology, (3) an immersive 3D view of the campus with animated energy flow — assessed against what is on `master` today (`9939b7b`) and what the 3D session left on `origin/claude/3d-site-visualization-gatc5z` (`f2649e3`, 2026-09-30).
Method: the two canvases and the campus study were read at the cited lines; the 3D branch's assessment, specs, plans and QA notes were read in full and its diff against master was trial-merged in a scratch worktree. Nothing here was run in a browser; the QA figures quoted are the branch's own, recorded in its notes.

This is an assessment, not a design. Its purpose is to say what exists, how far each layer is from the requirement, what the 3D branch still needs, where the real benefit beyond looks is, and which calls are the owner's.

## 0. Verdict

All three layers are viable, and two of them are much further along than the requirement assumes.

- **Layer 1, the abstract canvas, exists** (React Flow schematic, `pages/TopologyCanvas.tsx`). It does what the requirement asks at bus level. Its gaps for a *campus* reading are that assets are aggregated per bus and hidden by default, multi-port links (CHP, heat) are not drawn, and "flow animation" is colour bands plus a labelled arrow, not motion.
- **Layer 2, the map view, exists** (Leaflet, `pages/MapCanvas.tsx`), buses are draggable and dragging writes real coordinates back to the model. The requirement's "real infrastructure routing with real lengths" is **not** met: routed line waypoints are cosmetic, live only in the browser's localStorage, and lengths are straight-line haversine or typed by hand. There is no import of real routes or footprints. This is the layer with the most unbuilt product work and it is the cheapest to close.
- **Layer 3, the 3D view, is built**, not merely "in mind". The archived session "3D site visualization feasibility" (28–30 Sept) delivered a feasibility assessment, eleven owner decisions, a spike, and two full phases: a site entity drawn on the map, placement and drag in 3D, OSM footprints and terrain, a 21-type parametric asset library, five CC0 hero models, "as built / optimised" sizing, and **animated results** (storage gauges, rotors turning with output, halls glowing with load, chevrons marching along feeders and transformers in the flow direction). Its own QA passed 19/19 (Phase 1), 21/21 (Phase 2) and 2,490 frontend tests. **None of it is on master, no PR was opened, and the branch is 375 commits behind.** A trial merge onto today's master conflicts in only three files, so landing it is a one-to-two-week port, not a rewrite.
- **"AI-generated models" is the one part of the 3D ask that was never planned and that I recommend against for v1.** The branch's parametric-plus-curated approach is the right shape; generative 3D does not scale with `p_nom`, is not editable, and brings licensing uncertainty. AI earns its place in classifying assets and proposing layouts, not in producing meshes.
- **Benefit beyond visualisation is real but narrow**: the plot-fit check (land take vs boundary), seeing what the optimiser actually built, and the path to cable lengths and the campus electrical study. Those are the only places the 3D view changes a number. Everything else is communication value, which for a customer-facing planning tool is legitimate but should be named as such.

The recommended order is: land the 3D branch (it is the largest finished asset at risk of rotting), then close the map view's routing gap (because it is what makes both the map and the 3D view "real"), then decide on the owner questions in §7.

## 2. Layer 1 — the abstract canvas

### What exists (master)

- `pages/TopologyCanvas.tsx` (3,463 lines): buses as nodes coloured by voltage tier, lines / links / transformers as edges (transformer with the IEC two-circle symbol), user-routable edge waypoints with per-edge undo, auto-layout by voltage tier, minimap, collapsible legend.
- Positions are **latent**, deliberately decoupled from `bus.x`/`bus.y` (`TopologyCanvas.tsx:211-224`), stored in `layout.json` on the server and in the project bundle (`routers/projects.py:3424-3514`). This is exactly the "abstract layer, no real coordinates" the requirement describes, and the separation from the map's coordinates is by design, documented in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-30-unplaced-buses-map-design.md`.
- Results overlay via `components/CanvasResultsContext.tsx`: edge colour by loading band, a chip with |flow|, a direction arrow and loading %, per-bus generation/load donut, per-asset-group dispatch and SoC gauge. Time playback via `SnapshotPicker` (700 ms base tick).

### Gaps against R1

| Gap | Where | Why it matters for a campus |
|---|---|---|
| Generators, loads, storage are **aggregated** into at most four per-bus bubbles (Thermal / Renewables / Storage / Load) and are **hidden by default** (`TopologyCanvas.tsx:113-132`, `:2046`). | schematic | A campus reading is "electrolyser → H₂ store → offtake"; the schematic shows "Bus A → Bus B" with bubbles on request. The processes the owner names are not first-class nodes. |
| **Multi-port links are not drawn**: only `bus0 → bus1` appears; `bus2`/`bus3` never do. Separately, creating a three-port link drops `bus2` (`network_crud._drop_unknown_extras`, found by the 3D branch's smoke test). | schematic and map | A CHP's heat output or an electrolyser's waste heat vanish from every diagram. |
| "Flow animation" is **stepped recolouring**, not motion: no animated dash, particle or width-by-flow. Width is a static `s_nom` scaling (`:950`). | schematic and map | Fine for analysis; not the "energy flowing" look the owner describes. |
| Known defect: node positions can revert (`PUT /layout` before the first save returns 404; server copy then beats a newer local copy). Diagnosed, not fixed (`docs/superpowers/OPEN-ITEMS.md:105-118`). | schematic | Undermines trust in a hand-crafted campus diagram. |
| Nothing tests `runLayout`, `EditableEdge` or the overlay rendering. | tests | Changes here are unguarded. |

### Verdict on Layer 1

Viable and mostly done. To make it read as a campus process diagram rather than a grid schematic: a per-asset node mode (or asset groups shown by default for small networks), multi-port link rendering, and an animated-flow edge style are each small, contained changes on an existing canvas. Estimate: 1–3 weeks together, the multi-port fix being a backend one-liner plus rendering.

## 3. Layer 2 — the map view

### What exists (master)

- `pages/MapCanvas.tsx` (1,329 lines) over Esri World Imagery (satellite / hybrid, no key, no OSM base).
- `bus.x`/`bus.y` are longitude/latitude; (0,0) and non-finite values count as **unplaced** and are listed in `UnplacedBusesPanel` with a click-to-place mode (`utils/geo.ts`, `services/network_geometry.py:26-57`).
- **Bus markers are draggable and the drag writes back** to the model (`:742-763` → `PUT /api/network/buses/{name}`); the backend then recomputes the haversine length of every line touching that bus and offers an impedance rescale (`services/network_buses.py:40-70`). A "Recalculate line lengths" toolbar action does the same for all lines.
- Lines, links and transformers are drawn as straight chords plus optional user waypoints (`EditableLine`, `:382-570`). The results overlay is shared with the schematic.

### Gaps against R2

| Gap | Where | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Routed geometry is cosmetic and browser-local.** Waypoints live in `localStorage` only (`:107-143`), are not in `layout.json`, not in the bundle, not shared between users or machines; server persistence is marked "a deferred follow-up" (`:108-112`). | map | High. The owner's "real infrastructure between assets" cannot be stored, versioned or exported. |
| **Length never follows the route.** Lengths are user-typed (default 10 km from the palette, 1 km from the connect dialog) or haversine bus0→bus1; the code explicitly promises waypoints "NEVER touch line.length" (`:134-136`). Link lengths are never derived at all. | map / backend | High. "Real lengths based on how they actually run" is the stated requirement. |
| **No import of real topology.** No GeoJSON, KML, shapefile, OSM/Overpass or geocoding anywhere in the GUI; import covers NetCDF, CSV, Excel and MATPOWER only (`routers/io.py:55-290`). | backend | Medium. "Adoption from external sources" is unbuilt. The 3D branch adds an Overpass client for footprints, which is the natural seed. |
| **Assets have no coordinates**; only buses do. Asset bubbles are pixel offsets from the bus, stored in localStorage. A palette drop on the map yields a bus, never a coordinate (`hooks/useAssetDrag.ts:17-27`). | map | Medium. Mapping "generation and load to real positions" per asset is impossible in PyPSA's data model; the 3D branch's placement sidecar is where per-asset position lives. |
| **No create / connect tools on the map**; no legend, no minimap; transformers get no results overlay. | map | Low–medium. |
| Hub templates place campuses at placeholder coordinates (lon 2–3, lat 0–0.6; `project_templates/eh_templates.py:106-108`), so every template campus starts in the Gulf of Guinea. | templates | Low, but every demo trips on it. |
| Nothing tests `EditableLine`, bus dragging or the map overlay. | tests | Medium. |

### What "validated / compliant" can mean here

Two separate checks, both absent today:

1. **Geometric validity**: a routed line's stored length equals (or is within a tolerance of) its route length; a bus sits inside the site boundary; a transformer's two buses are co-located. All are pure functions over coordinates and are cheap to add once geometry is persisted.
2. **Electrical consistency**: a length change changes `r`, `x`, `b` — the existing rescale preview already does this on bus drag and is the right hook.

### Verdict on Layer 2

Viable; the drag-to-real-coordinates half is done, the real-routing half is not. The work is: a server-side routes sidecar (the `layout.json` pattern, which the 3D branch already generalised for its `sites.json`), an opt-in "length from route" rule with the rescale preview, GeoJSON/KML import of routes and site polygons, and multi-port link rendering shared with the schematic. Estimate: 3–5 weeks. This is the cheapest layer to bring to the requirement and the one the 3D view depends on to be "derived from the map" in a meaningful way.

## 4. Layer 3 — the 3D view

### 4.1 What the 3D session built

Branch `origin/claude/3d-site-visualization-gatc5z`: 46 commits, 145 files, +15,940 / −272, forked from master at `6e9fd4b` (2026-09-28), last commit 2026-09-30. No pull request exists for it.

**Decisions taken by the owner on 2026-09-28** (assessment §8a, eleven of them): purpose is *physical fit + communication*; a campus is a *cluster of buses* grouped by a *user-drawn boundary* on the Leaflet map; the transition is a *portal* (the map stays Leaflet; a site polygon opens a self-contained three.js scene), not a continuous zoom; realism from *open data* (OSM / terrain / Esri imagery), Google photoreal as a later paid option; "operate" means *edit parameters + animate solved results*, nothing SCADA-like; positions are *cosmetic in v1* but stored server-side so lengths and constraints can follow; *no paid services*; *parametric geometry plus 5–8 CC0 hero models*, no product-faithful equipment.

### 4.3 What remains, and what it costs

**A. Landing the branch (blocking everything else).**

- 375 commits behind master. A trial merge produced **three conflicting files**: `routers/projects.py` (two hunks: the `_BUNDLE_FILES` tuple gained `library_refs.json` on master and `sites.json` on the branch; `_BUNDLE_DIRS` gained `reports` vs `sites`; both are unions), `pypsa-gui/CONTEXT.md` (one hunk, additive) and `package-lock.json` (regenerate). 142 files auto-merge.
- Auto-merge is not correctness. The branch refactors `CanvasResultsContext.tsx` (an additive chunk-fetch variant) and `useAssetDrag.ts`; master has since changed the shell, the sidebar, the palette and the creation form (PRs #78, #83, #84, #88). Expect a day or two of test repair after the merge, then the full frontend and backend suites.
- The branch's Phase 1 note records the `_carry_sidecars_on_move` fix (D15), which master may have fixed differently since (`library_refs.json`, `reports/`). Reconcile, do not duplicate.
- **Estimate: 1–2 weeks** for merge, test repair, re-running the two headless QA drivers, and opening the PR.

**B. The open runbook steps the branch itself lists.**

- The **packaged macOS build has never shown the 3D view**; render path is WebGL2 (fine in WKWebView), but shadow quality and frame rate on a real GPU are unseen. One afternoon on a Mac.
- **Overpass was never exercised live** (unreachable from the build container; a stub answered every query). The public instance rate-limits; the setting `PYPSAGUI_OVERPASS_URL` exists for a private endpoint. Needs one real run on a real campus.
- Drops on a slope land short; buildings do not occlude clicks. Accepted for v1.

**C. Product defects the branch found but did not fix** (they are on master too; two are already addressed elsewhere):

- A–C: a network reset while a project is open lands in the session's scratch slot (undo reports success but changes nothing; a `.nc` import is silently lost or ignored). One root cause in `PyPSAService.reset_network` vs `active_project.resolve_for_session`. A patch and five regression tests sit beside the QA note (`docs/superpowers/notes/2026-09-30-full-e2e/`), not applied, needing a port to today's `routers/io.py`. **This is a data-loss class defect independent of 3D and should be triaged on its own.**
- D: second project from the same template → 409. Fixed on `claude/template-unique-name`; check whether it landed.
- `login.html` prefills a review credential and is served in auth mode.
- CHP creation drops `bus2`.

**D. Phase 3, never started, all optional**: Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles / Cesium ion context (paid, online-only), national LiDAR heights, glTF / 3D Tiles export, CC-BY transformer and switchgear heroes. 2–4 weeks each.

**E. Integration the requirement implies but no phase covered**: see §5 and §7.

### 4.4 "AI-generated models" — assessment

The owner asks whether 3D models can be generated by AI from the map data. Three readings, with a recommendation on each:

1. **Generative text/image-to-3D for the asset models** (a mesh of "a 40 ft BESS container" or "a 110/33 kV transformer"). Not recommended for v1. The output is a single static mesh: it cannot scale with `e_nom` the way the parametric library does (ten containers for 40 MWh, thirty when the optimiser picks 120 MWh), it has no named parts to animate (the branch spins a `blades` node and fills a gauge anchor), commercial licensing of generated assets varies by service and is unsettled, and a planning tool's credibility suffers more from an odd-looking transformer than from a plain one. If higher fidelity is wanted, the cheaper and safer path is a human choosing a handful of CC0/CC-BY models; the hero loader is model-agnostic (a manifest records each model's bounds, pivot and fit mode), so swapping the Kenney pack for better-looking models is a data change.
2. **AI-generated buildings for the site context.** Not needed. Footprints and heights from OSM, Overture or national LiDAR beat a generated guess for a real site, and the branch already draws them. Where open data is thin (the Gulf, India, Africa, per the assessment's region table), a generative "plausible industrial building" would still be a guess; a hill-shaded plot with blocky footprints is more honest in a planning document.
3. **AI driving the parametric library and layout.** Recommended, cheap, and consistent with the app's copilot. Examples: classify an oddly named component to an asset type when the carrier rule falls through; propose a site arrangement (loads near the hall, BESS near the transformer, PV on the roof) as placements the user accepts; draft a site boundary from an OSM landuse polygon (already planned in decision 2). These are tool calls writing into existing data structures, with the same guard rails as every other chat tool.

In short: AI should drive the *table*, not draw the *mesh*.

## 5. Benefit beyond visualisation

Honest answer: the 3D view changes a number in the study in exactly one place today, and it opens the door to two more.

| Benefit | Status | Where the value is |
|---|---|---|
| **Plot fit** — total asset land take (from the per-MW / per-MWh footprint table) against the boundary polygon area; assets outside the plot flagged red | Built (Phase 1, D9) | The only reading in which 3D answers "does 200 MWh of BESS fit next to the hall" — and it is the footprint table that answers it; the 3D scene is how the user sees and corrects it. The same check could be shown on the 2D map. |
| **Seeing what the optimiser built** — "Sized: as built / optimised" switch | Built (Phase 2, E6) | Makes the capacity-expansion result tangible for a customer; catches absurd results (a 30-container battery on a car park). |
| **Cable and feeder lengths from placement** → `length` on lines, `length_km` in the campus electrical YAML | Not built; decision 6 kept positions cosmetic in v1 but stored them so this can follow | This is where the three layers meet the gridspine campus study (§7). Today the campus draft uses 1 km when a line length is 0 and an assumed tie length. |
| **Catching model errors** | Side effect | The 3D view exposed the CHP `bus2` drop, the transformer-flow lookup bug in the schematic, and the stale-results-after-edit gap, because it draws exactly what the model holds. |
| **Customer communication** | Built | A planning tool's demo; the industry calibration (assessment §9) is that planning tools render parametric masses on real terrain and imagery, which is what this is. |
| **Engineering hand-off (BIM/IFC, vendor-accurate equipment)** | Out of scope by decision 1 | What Bentley and Cadence sell at enterprise prices. Not this tool. |

Everything else the 3D view does — glow, spin, chevrons — is presentation. That is not a criticism: the owner asked for an immersive, professional view and decision 1 named communication as half the purpose. But the business case should not claim analytical value the view does not have.

## 7. Decisions only the owner can make

1. **Land the 3D branch?** Every week unmerged costs merge effort and the chance that master's shell changes break it silently. Recommendation: yes, now, as the next GUI PR after the open campus PRs (#91, #92).
2. **Site vs campus vocabulary.** The 3D branch's glossary says **Site**, *avoid campus*; the gridspine stack says **campus** (an electrical study with a YAML single-line file and no geometry). They describe the same physical thing from two sides. Recommendation: keep "Site" for the drawn geometry and "campus study" for the electrical analysis, and record in `CONTEXT.md` that a campus study *runs on* a Site. Decide before the two meet in the UI.
3. **Should placement carry meaning in v2?** Lengths from routed lines and from 3D placements turn positions into modelled quantities: versioned, copied into scenarios, undone, locked. Decision 6 deferred this. It is the precondition for "real lengths" (R2) and for feeding the campus study. Recommendation: yes, as an explicit opt-in per project ("derive lengths from geometry"), never silently.
4. **Continuous zoom or portal, revisited.** Decision 3 chose the portal. R3's "zoom up to see the 3D rendering" reads as continuous. Recommendation: keep the portal and add a fly-in; continuous means replacing Leaflet with MapLibre and hand-writing a model layer (assessment §3), for presentation value only.
5. **Realism bar and model style.** Low-poly CC0 heroes versus higher-fidelity models versus photoreal paid tiles. Recommendation: look at the current scene on a real GPU first; swap heroes by data if needed; keep photoreal as a paid add-on.
6. **AI's role.** Generate meshes (not recommended) or drive classification, arrangement and boundaries (recommended, §4.4).
7. **Where the plot-fit and length checks should surface.** In 3D only, or also on the 2D map and in the campus study's compliance tables. Recommendation: wherever the number is used; the 3D view is one consumer.
